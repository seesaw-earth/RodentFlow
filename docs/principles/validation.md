# Validation

How close are the app's numbers to the truth? To answer that, we need videos where the truth is known exactly, frame by frame. This page reports such a test for every video assay, and is explicit about what it does and does not show.

## The test videos

Ten videos were generated by a program. Each shows a top-down mouse (body, head and a thin tail) moving through an arena according to a script of walking, exploring, resting, freezing and hiding. Because every frame was drawn by the script, the mouse's true position and state are known for every frame.

| Video | Assay | Length | Arena |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | Open field | 180 s | 40 × 40 cm square |
| 02 | Elevated plus maze | 160 s | Arms 30 × 5 cm |
| 03 | Light–dark box | 150 s | 60 × 30 cm, dark third covered |
| 04 | Place preference, 3 chambers | 150 s | 90 × 30 cm |
| 05 | Novel object recognition (test) | 180 s | 40 × 40 cm |
| 06 | Water maze, acquisition trial | 34 s | 100 cm pool |
| 07 | Water maze, probe trial | 60 s | 100 cm pool |
| 08 | Barnes maze | 30 s | 92 cm, 20 holes |
| 09 | Fear conditioning, cue test (CS at 120 and 210 s) | 270 s | 30 × 25 cm |
| 10 | Y-maze | 83 s | Arms 35 × 10 cm |

All videos are 1280 × 720 at 30 fps, H.264, the format an iPhone records.

- **Ground truth.** The true position is the center of the drawn mouse pixels in centimeters. The true metrics were computed by a separate Python program from these positions and the scripted states, using the definitions in this manual and the same 10 Hz time points.
- **App values.** The videos were processed by the app's own Swift tracking and metric code (version 1.3), with the arena calibrated exactly. Decoding mimics the iPhone: 10 Hz, frames at 192 px width, background from 21 frames.

## What the numbers say

- **Counts and sequences match exactly in every video.** This covers center, arm and chamber entries, transitions, platform crossings, Barnes errors, hole-visit sequence and search strategy, the Y-maze arm sequence, and the number of freezing bouts.
- **Times and latencies** are within 0.6 s, most within 0.2 s. **Percentages** are within 0.5 percentage points. The discrimination index is 0.416 against 0.415.
- **Freezing**: CS freezing is 83.0 % against 83.3 %, and the mean bout length is within 0.1 s.
- **Distance** is slightly overestimated (+1 % to +2 %) in arenas that fill the frame, because residual tracking jitter adds to the true path.
- **Plus maze distance is the exception: +35 %.** The maze covers only about 55 % of the frame width, i.e. about 1.5 pixels per cm at analysis resolution. Small changes of the body outline while the mouse sniffs in place shift the centroid by a pixel or two, and over 160 s this adds up. Times and entries on the same video are exact. Use plus-maze distance only to compare groups filmed the same way, or frame the maze tightly.

## What this test does not show

Synthetic videos are cleaner than real ones: even light, no reflections, no bedding, no shadows, a uniform floor. They verify that the tracking pipeline, the calibration geometry and every metric definition work as specified. They do not replace a check on your own setup. Before relying on a new setup, especially for freezing thresholds and the object and hole radii, score 3–5 of your own videos by hand and compare them with the app.

A second limit is in the definitions themselves: the app scores the **body center**. Hand scoring of object exploration or hole pokes uses the **nose**. The default radii (8 cm) are chosen to bridge that difference for a mouse, but the two kinds of scoring measure slightly different things.
