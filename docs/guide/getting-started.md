# Quick start

This walk-through analyses one open-field video. Every other task follows the same steps; only the handles you place and the measures differ.

## On iPhone or iPad {#phone}

1. **Open the Assay Library** and choose **Open field** (or tap **New analysis** on Home and pick it from the list). The library groups tests by area: social, navigation, anxiety, memory, motor, general, then classic centroid and multi-chamber tail suspension. Each test has a page with what it measures.
2. **Choose the video** from Photos or Files, and the **coat colour** of the mouse: *Dark* (the default) or *White*.
3. **Fill in the animal**: animal ID, group, sex, and phase, day and trial if the test has them. These fields are what the Groups tab uses later.
4. **Calibrate**: on the first frame, drag the corner handles onto the inside corners of the arena and enter its width and height in centimetres. Other tasks add their own handles (cups, objects, maze arms, a platform, a shelter); each handle is labelled.
5. **Detect**: tap **Start detection**. Only the arena (plus a small margin) is analysed. You can keep using the phone; progress is saved, and a paused analysis continues where it stopped.
6. **Review**: play the video with the six keypoints drawn on the mouse. The timeline under it shows each scored behaviour; flagged moments (for example *Outside the calibrated arena*) are marked so you can check them.
7. **Results**: distance, centre time, entries, immobility and the other measures, each with its definition; the track, the heat map and the time course. Tap **Export data** for tables and figures.

Repeat for every animal. When a few are done, open **Groups**, choose the task and a measure, and read the comparison: see [Comparing groups](./groups.md).

## On the Mac {#mac}

1. **New task…**, choose **Open field**, and name it (for example “Open field – cohort 3”).
2. **Add videos** by dragging them onto the task. Each video is one session of one animal: give it an animal ID, recording date and session number; groups and sex belong to the animal.
3. **Calibrate** one video, then apply the calibration to the others filmed with the same camera position.
4. **Analyse**: the Mac tracks the videos one after another and saves as it goes, so a long recording survives a pause or a restart.
5. **Review & export** each session, then **Compare groups** for the whole task.

The [Mac quick start](../mac/quick-start.md) goes through the Mac step by step.

## Before your real experiment {#before}

- Record a short test video in your real setup and analyse it first; [Recording videos that track well](./recording.md) lists what matters most.
- Use the same settings for every animal of a study. The settings used are saved with each result and written into the Methods text.
- For two-mouse tasks, use mice that differ in coat colour or mark one of them; see [Dyadic social interaction](../tasks/dyadic-social.md#setup).
